\section{Saved Error Decomposition and a Fixed Feasibility Screen}
\label{supp:stochastic-checks}
This section reports analyses of existing records. It adds no training, future branches, or revised source predictions. These descriptive calculations do not instantiate the strict conditions of Proposition~\ref{prop:stoch-prefix}.

\subsection{Eight-stream prediction errors}
For a fixed source, task, ratio, representation, and horizon, let $p$ be the frozen prediction and $y_i$, $1\le i\le8$, its saved future changes. The exact identity is
\begin{equation}
 \frac18\sum_i(y_i-p)^2=(\bar y-p)^2
                    +\frac18\sum_i(y_i-\bar y)^2.
\end{equation}
With sample variance $s_y^2$ using denominator seven, the sample-corrected squared conditional-mean error is $(\bar y-p)^2-s_y^2/8$. Negative estimates are retained. Full inputs, formulas, and summaries are in
\begin{center}
\path{experiments/results/empirical/task_motion_error_decomposition/summary.json}.
\end{center}
The corresponding CSV files retain every source/task/ratio/horizon cell. Development and validation stages remain separate; tasks, ratios, horizons, and streams are paired measurements, not additional independent seed replicates.

For validation square-model predefined band tasks at eight steps, corrected mean-error contributions divided by MSE are $31.4\%,30.6\%,39.2\%$ for the encoder and $35.6\%,31.8\%,52.0\%$ for the projection at updates $500,2000,3500$, respectively. Estimates use eight streams and are noisy. The interval component named noise occupies $92$--$98\%$ of projection half-width, whereas the observed between-stream population-variance term occupies $42$--$61\%$ of MSE. These are different estimands and units; neither fraction identifies a causal noise contribution. Sixty-four-step results lie outside the declared eight-step calibration support and remain diagnostic only.

\subsection{One source fixed before future inspection}
The feasibility screen fixes development seed zero, the square model at update $500$, the predefined task \texttt{x\_low}, ratio one, and horizons $1{:}8$. The records already existed; this is a retrospective screen fixed before reinspection, not a prospectively registered validation. Encoder and projection are both retained, with $\alpha=0.05$ separately for each; no joint $95\%$ claim is made. The original algorithm uses learning rate $0.002$, batch size $256$, sixteen fit batches, thirty-two error batches, four eight-step source probes, and eight saved future streams. No algorithm setting is changed to find a window. The complete selection, original settings, comparisons, and missing conditions are in
\begin{center}
\path{experiments/results/theory/stochastic_window_feasibility.json}.
\end{center}

For a unit task, $0\le R\le1$ for every orthogonal row projector, including rank changes. Conditional on the current state, $D=R_{j+1}-R_j$ has range width one, so $|D-\E_jD|\le1$ is justified without bounding Gaussian parameter gradients. Retaining this global increment scale $c=1$, a conservative finite-horizon Bernstein--Freedman envelope is
\begin{align}
 B_H(v)&=\sqrt{2vx}+2cx/3,\\
 x&=\log(2H/\alpha).
\end{align}
At $H=8$, even $v=0$ gives radius $3.84554733$. For the finite-$\lambda$ boundary in Equation~\eqref{eq:stoch-lambda-boundary}, every finite grid has $J\ge1$ and $\lambda<3/c$, hence its zero-variance radius is strictly larger than
\begin{equation}
 \frac{c\log(2/\alpha)}3=1.22962648.
\end{equation}
The largest frozen source centers over the specified prefixes have absolute magnitude only $5.45943\,10^{-5}$ for the encoder and $3.48344\,10^{-5}$ for the projection. Thus these particular $c=1$ envelopes cannot exclude zero even after optimistically setting calibration uncertainty, future variance, mean drift, finite-step error, and tube penalties to zero. The frozen slope is merely the stipulated idealized center; it has not been independently established as the exact finite-step conditional mean.

The saved eight-stream variances are descriptive plugin values, not variance upper confidence bounds. A deterministic increment could instead permit $c=0$; a proved tighter local range or conditional moment-generating-function bound could also change the result. The screen therefore rejects this global-range instantiation, not all possible stochastic certificates or actual predictability. It gives a reason to stop the present numerical instantiation without adding calibration batches, training, or future streams. No strict stochastic window is claimed.
