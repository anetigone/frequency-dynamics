\section{Discussion and Limitations}
The structural question has two parts: whether a training update leaves the current representation subspace, and whether that exterior movement intersects task-predictive directions. The local moment identity and regression formula make both parts explicit without assuming independent frequencies. The near-conservation decomposition separates data/objective anisotropy from parameter-mobility anisotropy, while the finite displacement bound identifies task cross-block mass and normal movement as separate limits on risk amplitude.

The solvable bottleneck family explains rebound as passage through a task-aligned direction, followed by continued movement. Its fixed gaps hold for both encoder and projection across a task cone and a structured covariance interval. Theorem~\ref{thm:asymmetric-rebound} supplies a separate quantitative stability result at fixed data and rates: all six initialization coordinates vary independently in a box of radius $8\,10^{-6}$, retaining positive improvement and rebound gaps at common angle sections. The proof controls transverse imbalance, angular rotation, and task projectors, without requiring scale coordinates to remain close. The neighborhood is small; it does not establish typicality, a random-initialization probability, or joint robustness to arbitrary covariance and augmentation perturbations. The symmetric family's rate exclusion concerns the ordered subspace path, not equal risk at common energy or update budget.

The finite-window inequalities provide a falsifiable certificate rather than a promise of useful control. A small task cross block suppresses first-order transfer; small normal movement limits total transfer; a small competition margin can be overwhelmed by drift, gradient error, or discrete curvature. Generic source bounds can be empty or too conservative. The interval calculation is nonempty on a stored positive construction and predicts later changes at a common energy section. It uses a known population task, a deterministic vector field, and short windows. It does not establish useful path-budget constants for arbitrary states, stochastic optimization, or natural-image OLS. The analysis was designed after the trajectories existed, with constants calculated from source states before future CSV records were read.

The earlier narrow-box certificate remains a computer-assisted proof of a larger projection-risk rebound, not an encoder-loss theorem or a probability statement. The scale equivalence belongs to established work on initialization imbalance. The generic regression, projector calculus, and Taylor remainder are connecting tools; their presence alone is not a novelty claim. A theorem-level comparison of the new constructive family and task-geometry criteria with the closest literature remains necessary before a submission-level contribution judgment.

Empirical textures retain a clear conditional correspondence and a boundary: geometry is informative early, but finite data induce reproducible linear drift and late predictions weaken. Independent future streams show useful early frozen-source predictions; the sparse encoder coverage prevents treating this as a general encoder-risk guarantee. The eight-stream decomposition retains both mean-offset and dispersion terms. Their source cannot be identified from empirical noise-budget proportions. The conditional stochastic theorem instead separates source estimation, drift, curvature, and centered task variance. It uses the orientation of normal noise and retains quadratic leakage, including noise-induced mean loss at zero task cross block. It does not establish nonempty strict windows on these Gaussian-augmentation runs: valid support/tail and variance confidence bounds remain to be instantiated. Under exact isotropy, ordinary discrete linear gradient descent also preserves its encoder row space as long as rank is retained, so step size alone cannot explain its empirical drift.

CIFAR-10 and DTD remain useful negative evidence. Better supervised low-rank heads expose class information already present at initialization; dominant-statistic removal does not yield clear encoder class gains; rare local risk reversals are tiny and short-lived. None of those observations by itself identifies the new bound's controlling quantity. A direct natural-image test would need source-calibrated task coupling and motion/error bounds checked against separate future states or update streams. We have not run that test, and have not resumed large-scale training. Weak class gains therefore remain an unresolved transfer boundary, not an explained success of the theory.

\section{Conclusion}
Task risk changes through exterior subspace motion coupled to the task matrix. The constructive encoder rebound persists on an explicit neighborhood outside exact symmetric initialization. Task-projected stochastic geometry supplies conditional finite-prefix envelopes that separate mean error from centered fluctuation. Source-fixed texture forecasts establish early empirical nonvacuity and a later boundary; they do not validate the strict stochastic conditions or explain weak natural-image transfer.
